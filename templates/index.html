01 — Foundations

What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence is the science of building machines that can perform tasks which normally need human thinking — recognizing images, understanding language, making decisions, and finding patterns in data.

AI isn't one single thing. It's an umbrella that covers Machine Learning (systems that improve from data instead of fixed rules), Deep Learning (layered neural networks inspired loosely by the brain), and Generative AI (models like Claude and GPT that create text, images, code, and more).

Instead of being told exactly what to do step by step, an AI system is shown examples and learns the underlying pattern on its own.

02 — The Process

How AI Actually Works

Every AI model, from a spam filter to a chatbot, is built the same core way — through this four-stage sequence.

01

Data Collection

Massive amounts of text, images, or numbers are gathered. This is the raw material — the more relevant and clean the data, the smarter the resulting model.

02

Training

The data is fed through a neural network. The model makes a guess, checks how wrong it was, and adjusts millions (or billions) of internal values called weights. This repeats billions of times.

03

Learning Patterns

Over time the network stops memorizing and starts recognizing structure — grammar, objects, logic, style — the same way a person builds intuition through practice.

04

Inference

The trained model is deployed. When you type a question, it uses everything it learned to predict the best possible response, in a fraction of a second.

03 — The Landscape

The Big Players in AI

A handful of companies are driving most of today's progress in artificial intelligence.

OpenAI

Creator of the GPT model family and ChatGPT, one of the products that brought generative AI into mainstream use.

Anthropic

Builder of the Claude family of models, with a strong focus on AI safety and making models genuinely helpful and reliable.

Google DeepMind

Google's AI research lab, known for the Gemini models and landmark work like AlphaFold, which predicts protein structures.

Meta AI

Develops the open-weight Llama model family, pushing large chunks of frontier AI research into the open.

xAI

Elon Musk's AI company, building the Grok model family and massive training infrastructure like the Colossus supercomputer.

Microsoft

A major investor and infrastructure partner in the AI space, embedding AI across Windows, Office, and Azure cloud services.

NVIDIA

Doesn't build chatbots — builds the GPUs that train almost every major model, making it one of the most critical companies in AI.

DeepSeek

A Chinese AI lab known for releasing highly capable, efficiently trained open-weight models at a fraction of typical cost.

04 — The Skill Behind It

What Is Coding, Really?

Coding is how humans give precise instructions to a computer. A computer doesn't guess what you mean — it does exactly what the code says, so coding is really the skill of thinking in exact, logical steps.

Languages like Python are especially popular for AI because they read almost like plain English and have huge libraries built specifically for machine learning.

You don't need to be a genius to start coding. Every expert began with a single line — usually one that just prints a message to the screen.

05 — Extra Insight

Why Learn AI Right Now?

It's the new literacy

Just like reading, writing, and using a computer became essential skills, understanding AI is becoming a basic requirement across almost every career.

The barrier is lower than ever

You no longer need a PhD to build with AI. Free tools, open models, and simple APIs let anyone build a working AI app in a single afternoon.

It multiplies your other skills

A designer who knows AI, a writer who knows AI, a student who knows AI — all become dramatically more capable than peers who don't.

You shape it, or it shapes you

AI is already influencing hiring, education, and creative work. Understanding how it works puts you in control of that shift instead of just reacting to it.
